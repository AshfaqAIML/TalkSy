# TalkSy — Product Vision

> From your first English word to confident communication.

## One-liner

TalkSy is a premium, structured English learning platform for second-language
learners that takes a complete beginner to confident, real-world communication
through a CEFR-aligned curriculum with genuine practice in reading, writing,
listening and speaking — and honest, server-authoritative progress tracking.

## The problem

Most English learning products fail learners in one of three ways:

1. **Shallow gamification.** Apps optimised for streaks and notifications teach
   recognition, not production. Learners tap correct answers for months and
   still cannot hold a two-minute conversation.
2. **Unstructured content.** Video libraries and grammar references have no
   ordered path. Learners don't know what to study next, gaps go unnoticed, and
   motivation decays.
3. **Fake progress signals.** Badges and percentage bars disconnected from real
   ability. Learners cannot tell what they actually know.

## The TalkSy answer

| Problem | TalkSy response |
| --- | --- |
| Recognition ≠ production | Every lesson mixes all four skills; speaking and writing are first-class, graded exercises — not stickers. |
| No path | A strictly ordered curriculum: level → unit → lesson, sequentially unlocked, with a visible CEFR ladder (A1 → C1). |
| Fake progress | XP, accuracy and vocabulary strength are computed **server-side from real answers** and can never be inflated by the client. |

## Product principles

1. **Honest progress.** If a number is shown to a learner, it is derived from
   real, recorded work. Speaking practice never pretends to be a quiz score.
2. **One clear next step.** At any moment the learner knows exactly what to do
   next: the next unlocked lesson, the weakest words to review, today's goal.
3. **Skills, not screens.** Design decisions are judged by whether they produce
   reading, writing, listening or speaking practice — never by time-on-app.
4. **Respect the learner.** Simple-English explanations, no dark patterns, no
   artificial paywalls on core learning, graceful fallbacks when a browser
   feature (e.g. speech recognition) is unavailable.
5. **Content as craft.** All curriculum material is original, level-appropriate
   and versioned in code — reviewed like any other source, not scraped or
   licensed filler.

## Target learners

- **Primary:** adults and young adults (16+) studying English as a second
  language, from absolute beginners (A1) to independent users (B2/C1), studying
  self-paced without a tutor.
- **Secondary:** classroom learners who want structured practice and measurable
  homework between lessons.
- **Not (yet) the target:** children under 13, learners needing certification
  exam prep (IELTS/TOEFL-specific modules are a future decision), or
  enterprise/school administration deployments.

## What success looks like

- A learner who completes A1 can introduce themselves, handle greetings,
  numbers, family, shopping, directions and simple plans — and has *spoken* and
  *written* English doing it.
- A learner can answer "what do I actually know?" with real numbers: words at
  each strength level, accuracy per exercise type, days actually studied.
- The full A1→C1 ladder exists, with a placement path that starts learners at
  the right height instead of forcing everyone through lesson one.

## Out of scope (explicitly)

- Social feeds, avatars and cosmetic gamification (leaderboards are planned but
  subordinate to learning).
- Live tutoring marketplace.
- Native mobile apps (the web app is responsive and installs as a PWA-grade
  experience; native shells add nothing yet).

## Naming

The product is **TalkSy** (from "talk"). The former working name *EnglishPath*
is retired; any reference to it in old material means TalkSy.
